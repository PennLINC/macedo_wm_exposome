# White matter reflects the childhood exposome

Replication guide: https://pennlinc.github.io/macedo_wm_exposome/

## Abstract
The childhood environment is critical for brain development. However, most neuroimaging studies examine individual or a limited set of environmental measures, obscuring how the combination of complex, real-world exposures jointly influence brain development. Here we investigated how white matter shape and microstructure are linked to a general exposome factor, primarily reflecting socioeconomic inequality and related factors. Using multi-shell diffusion MRI from 8,183 children (ages 9-10) in the ABCD study, we quantified 21 features across 62 person-specific white matter tracts. The general exposome factor showed widespread and highly replicable associations with white matter: more advantaged environments were associated with larger tract macrostructure and lower orientation dispersion. Exposome-white matter association strength increased with sensorimotor-association axis range, such that tracts connecting cortical regions farther apart along the cortical hierarchy showed stronger associations.  Multivariate models demonstrated that patterns of white matter features explained 25% of the variance in the exposome in unseen individuals. Notably, white matter-based prediction of cognition was markedly reduced after accounting for the general exposome factor (~82% reduction in explained variance), indicating that brain-cognition associations overlap substantially with measures of socioeconomic context. These findings generalized to independent data from the Healthy Brain Network (n=869). Together, these results suggest that the childhood environment is reflected in white matter architecture in late childhood. 

Keywords: white matter, diffusion MRI, neuroimaging, neurodevelopment, exposome, environment, cognition, hierarchy, macrostructure, microstructure, NODDI
